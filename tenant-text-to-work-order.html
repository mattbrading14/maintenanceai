Maintenance requests

How to turn a tenant text into a maintenance work order

Start with the message you already have. Record the property and unit, what the tenant noticed, and when they reported it. Verify the priority and arrange access before assigning the job. Leave unknown details blank rather than guessing.

Download the editable work-order template

What to capture before you assign the work

  1. Save only what you need. If you take a screenshot, crop out unrelated messages and redact access codes and other sensitive information. Give the vendor the information needed for the job, not the full conversation.
  2. Write down what the tenant reported. Log the property, suite or unit, exact location, observed issue, and when the message arrived. Record when the issue started only if the tenant said when.
  3. Review and assign. Confirm urgency using your building’s procedures. Arrange access separately, pick the right trade, and ask the vendor to inspect the reported issue rather than presenting a guess as a diagnosis.
  4. Close the loop. Confirm the work was done, record the actual completion date and outcome, and keep the service report or invoice with your building records.

For an active emergency, follow your building’s emergency procedures immediately rather than waiting for a software draft.

Tenant text

“The break-room sink in Suite 204 leaks underneath when we run the faucet.”

Draft work order · review before sending

Location
Suite 204 break room. Confirm the property.
Reported issue
Water leaks under the sink when the faucet runs.
Next action
Confirm access with the tenant, then ask a plumber to inspect and identify the source.
Still to verify
When it started, whether it leaks with the faucet off, and whether nearby materials are affected.
